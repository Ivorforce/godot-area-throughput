Flow

PRs opened vs PRs closed (merged or not) per month. The band between the lines is the monthly imbalance: red while more opens than closes (backlog growing), green while closing outpaces opening (backlog shrinking). Hover for the exact net.

Resolution rate

Of the PRs opened each month, the share closed — merged or not — within each time span, layered from within-a-day (darkest, bottom) to within-32-months (lightest, top). Thicker dark layers = faster decisions; the white gap above the top layer is the share still open. Sinking layers mean newer PRs wait longer. These are shares, not volumes — they can sink even while total closes grow, if intake grows faster; the Flow chart above has the absolute numbers, and the tooltip shows each layer's counts. Layers step down before the present on purpose: a month that hasn't yet had a span's full length can't be judged against it.

Caveats
  • Labels are read as they are today. Renamed labels keep their full history (they're tracked by ID); deleted labels keep their last-known name; each label's charts start at its first-ever PR.
  • A label's stats cover every PR carrying it, whatever the PR's main subject. Cross-cutting labels that ride along on other areas' PRs (documentation, usability, …) therefore describe "PRs touching this" — largely reviewed, blocked, and resolved on the primary area's behalf — not a dedicated team's service.
  • These are throughput statistics, not performance reviews. They cannot see review depth, mentoring, design discussions, issue triage, or any work outside PR reviews — comparing individuals by these numbers alone misreads what they measure.
  • All series apply today's labels to yesterday's PRs — treat early history as an approximation.
  • Resolution-rate layers stop before the present on purpose: a month that hasn't yet had a span's full length isn't judged against it, rather than shown misleadingly fast.
  • The 3-mo average on the resolution chart combines the three months' PRs into one pool and computes the rate over that pool — averaging three percentages would let a tiny month count as much as a busy one.
  • Decisions credited to a person are approvals, change requests, and closing someone else's PR unmerged. Merging is not separately credited — the release team merges what reviewers approved, so the approval is the decision. Nor is authoring: however central someone's own PRs are to an area, these numbers track who processes incoming work, not who writes the code.
  • Draft PRs count as opened; bot reviews and authors' replies to review threads are excluded everywhere.
  • The current partial month is excluded from all series.
  • Post-merge reviews count in the month they were submitted, so reviewed can slightly lag merged.

Data updates monthly via GitHub Actions. Raw data, scripts, and metric definitions live in Ivorforce/godot-area-throughput; aggregates are at data/index.json.
