Flow

PRs opened vs PRs closed per month, with the closes split by outcome: merged (blue fill) vs closed without merging — rejected, superseded, or withdrawn (gray fill). The band between the lines is the monthly imbalance: red while more opens than closes (backlog growing), green while closing outpaces opening (backlog shrinking). The split shows outcome, not quality: an unmerged close is a decision too — a considered rejection or a superseded PR serves the area just as a merge does. Hover for exact counts and the net.

Resolution rate

Of the PRs opened each month, the share closed — merged or not — within each time span, layered from within-a-day (darkest, bottom) through within-32-months, topped by closed-to-date (lightest). The white gap above the top layer is exact: of the PRs opened that month, the share still open as of the last data update. Thicker dark layers = faster decisions; sinking layers mean newer PRs wait longer. Washed-out bands are months still younger than the span: they count the closures so far and can only grow — part of the sinking toward the right edge is just spans that haven't finished counting, and each span washes out at its own month, longest first. These are shares, not volumes — they can sink even while total closes grow, if intake grows faster; the Flow chart above has the absolute numbers, and the tooltip shows each layer's counts.

Caveats
  • Labels are read as they are today. Renamed labels keep their full history (they're tracked by ID); deleted labels keep their last-known name; each label's charts start at its first-ever PR.
  • A label's stats cover every PR carrying it, whatever the PR's main subject. Cross-cutting labels that ride along on other areas' PRs (documentation, usability, …) therefore describe "PRs touching this" — largely reviewed, blocked, and resolved on the primary area's behalf — not a dedicated team's service.
  • These are throughput statistics, not performance reviews. They cannot see review depth, mentoring, design discussions, issue triage, or any work outside PR reviews — comparing individuals by these numbers alone misreads what they measure.
  • All series apply today's labels to yesterday's PRs — treat early history as an approximation.
  • Washed-out resolution-rate bands are lower bounds, not estimates: a month younger than a span shows its closures so far, and the band can only grow until the span completes and it turns solid. The closed-to-date layer and the white gap above it are exact as of the data snapshot.
  • Several washed-out bands can be filling in at once: a month's PRs differ in age by up to a month — up to three with the 3-mo average, which pools neighboring cohorts — so one point can straddle more than one span window. Spans no pooled PR has reached yet coincide with closed-to-date and collapse to zero width.
  • The 3-mo average on the resolution chart combines the three months' PRs into one pool and computes the rate over that pool — averaging three percentages would let a tiny month count as much as a busy one.
  • Decisions credited to a person are approvals, change requests, and closing someone else's PR unmerged. Merging is not separately credited — the release team merges what reviewers approved, so the approval is the decision. Nor is authoring: however central someone's own PRs are to an area, these numbers track who processes incoming work, not who writes the code.
  • Draft PRs count as opened; bot reviews and authors' replies to review threads are excluded everywhere.
  • The current partial month is excluded from all series.
  • Linked counts open the matching GitHub search — always a queue of open PRs, never a person's activity record. Live search results drift from the monthly snapshot; numbers no search can express stay unlinked.
  • Post-merge reviews count in the month they were submitted, so reviewed can slightly lag merged.

Data updates monthly via GitHub Actions. Raw data, scripts, and metric definitions live in Ivorforce/godot-area-throughput; aggregates are at data/index.json.
